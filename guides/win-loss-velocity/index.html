Win/Loss & Velocity: what to do with what you see

Monthly rhythm

Most of these reports look back 12 months, so they move slowly. Weekly checks won't show much. A monthly review and a deeper quarterly one work better.

  • Monthly (about 30 minutes): open the Win-Loss & Velocity dashboard. Look at loss reasons first, then win rates, then sales cycle and deal size.
  • Quarterly: go through Stage Exits and the deal math below, and decide on one change to your sales process for next quarter.
  • One-on-ones: each rep reviews My Losses This Quarter with their manager.

Win Rate by Rep

Each rep's share of closed deals that were won over the last 12 months. Always read it next to how many deals each rep closed: 90% on 3 deals means less than 40% on 50.

What to do

  • A low win rate is a starting point, not a verdict. Look at that rep's loss reasons and where their deals drop out in Stage Exits before deciding what to coach.
  • A very high win rate with low volume often means a rep only creates opportunities once a deal is nearly certain. That hides real pipeline and makes forecasting harder. Agree on the point where a deal becomes an opportunity, and hold everyone to it.

Win Rate by Source

Win rate for closed deals by lead source. It shows which channels bring deals that actually close, which isn't always the same as the channels bringing the most leads.

What to do

  • Put more time and budget behind sources that win, and question sources that bring volume but rarely close.
  • If you also have the Leads & Top of Funnel pack, compare this with Lead Conversion by Source. A source that converts leads well but loses deals is bringing in the wrong buyers.
  • A "-" bar means deals with no Lead Source. If it's large, make Lead Source required on opportunities, and check that it carries over when leads convert.

Win Rate by Type

Win rate by opportunity type, such as new business against existing customers.

What to do

  • Deals with existing customers usually win at a higher rate than new business. If yours don't, look at how accounts are managed after the first sale.
  • A "-" bar means deals with no Type. Make Type required so this report stays useful.

Loss Reasons

Why deals were lost over the last 12 months. The pack requires a reason on every lost deal, so this is the most direct feedback your sales process gets. What matters is which reason is biggest, because each one points somewhere different.

What to do

  • Price: check where these deals were lost in Stage Exits. Losing on price at the proposal stage usually means value wasn't established earlier, or budget wasn't qualified. Look at discounting habits too.
  • Lost to Competitor: find out which competitor and why. If it's the same one again and again, write down how your team should position against them.
  • No Decision / Status Quo: the buyer didn't feel enough pain to change. This is a qualification and urgency problem, not a closing problem.
  • Budget or Timing: not always a real loss. Set a follow-up task for when the budget or timing changes instead of forgetting the account.
  • Unresponsive: often means the deal depended on one contact. Push reps to involve more than one person on each deal.
  • Other: if this is one of the biggest slices, the reasons don't match what your team hears. Edit the Loss Reason (RP) picklist. The install guide shows how.

Watch for

Reasons picked to get past the required field. If one reason shows up on almost every loss for a rep, ask about a few of those deals directly.

Average Sales Cycle

Average days from opportunity creation to closed won, by rep and deal type, over the last 12 months. Long cycles tie up rep time and push revenue out.

What to do

  • Use it to sanity-check close dates. If your average cycle is 60 days and a deal created this week has a close date two weeks out, ask what's different about it.
  • Compare cycles by deal type. Existing customers usually close faster than new business. Use the right number for each when setting expectations.
  • Big differences between reps usually come from deal size or from when each rep creates the opportunity, not from speed. Check both before drawing conclusions.

Average Deal Size

Average won deal amount by fiscal quarter over the last 12 months.

What to do

  • A falling line can mean heavier discounting, smaller customers, or a shift toward smaller products. Check whether Price is also growing in Loss Reasons.
  • A rising line means you're winning bigger customers or selling more per deal. Find out which deals drove it so you can do it on purpose.

Stage Exits

Every stage change in the last 90 days, showing where each deal moved from and to. It's the report that shows where deals actually advance, stall, go backward or drop out.

What to do

  • Find the stage where the most deals go to Closed Lost. That's where your process breaks down. Fixing that one stage usually matters more than anything else in this pack.
  • Watch for deals moving backward. A few is normal. A lot from one stage usually means deals were moved forward before they were ready.
  • Compare how many deals leave each stage forward against how many leave it to Closed Lost. That ratio is your stage-to-stage conversion.

Putting the numbers together

Win rate, deal size and sales cycle together tell you how much pipeline you need. A simple version:

  • Deals you need to win = revenue target ÷ average deal size.
  • Deals you need in pipeline = deals you need to win ÷ win rate.
  • When they need to exist = at least one average sales cycle before the end of the period.

For example, a $300,000 quarterly target with a $25,000 average deal and a 25% win rate means about 12 wins, which takes about 48 qualified opportunities. With a 60-day cycle, most of those need to be in the pipeline in the first month of the quarter. If they aren't, the quarter is already short.

Rep-Level Personal reports

My Won Deals This Quarter and My Losses This Quarter show each rep only their own results.

What to do

  • In one-on-ones, go through My Losses This Quarter and agree on one thing to do differently for each loss. Keep it short and specific.
  • Look at My Won Deals for what went right. Wins teach as much as losses, and reps hear about them less.
