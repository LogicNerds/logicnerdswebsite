Daily and weekly rhythm

Leads go cold in hours, so this pack needs a faster rhythm than the others.

  • Reps, every morning: clear My Untouched Leads. Every new lead gets a first touch the same day it comes in.
  • Manager, weekly (about 15 minutes): open the Leads & Top of Funnel dashboard. Check speed to lead by rep and the untouched count, then lead aging.
  • Monthly: review conversion by source with whoever owns marketing or lead buying.

Speed to Lead

Average hours from when a lead is created to its first completed activity, by owner, for leads created in the last 90 days. Only leads that have been touched are counted. The rest are on the Untouched Leads list.

What to do

  • Set a target response time and share it with the team. Measured in hours, not days.
  • It matters more than most teams think. In a Harvard Business Review study of online leads, companies that tried to contact a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to have a meaningful conversation with a key decision maker as companies that tried even an hour later.
  • If one rep is much slower than the rest, check whether they have too many leads or are logging the first touch late. Then rebalance or coach.
  • Look at when slow leads come in. If most arrive in the evening or on weekends, the fix is coverage or routing, not effort.

Watch for

The pack records the first completed activity logged on the lead. A call that isn't logged doesn't count, and a call logged a day later counts as a day late. Speed to Lead also only measures leads created after the pack was installed.

Untouched Leads

Open leads with no logged activity at all, oldest first. Every one is a lead someone paid to get that nobody has followed up on.

What to do

  • Reps clear their own list daily. Managers check the team total weekly.
  • Reassign leads that sit untouched for more than a few days. An old lead in a busy rep's queue won't get worked.
  • Leads that were never worth working, like spam or duplicates, still count here until they're closed. Set them to your disqualified status so they drop off.

Lead Conversion by Source

The percentage of leads from each source that converted into a contact or opportunity, for leads created in the last 12 months. It shows which channels produce real buyers, not just lead volume.

What to do

  • Put more budget and effort behind sources with high conversion. Question sources that bring lots of leads but few conversions, especially ones you pay for.
  • If you have the Win/Loss & Velocity pack, compare with Win Rate by Source. The best sources both convert and win.
  • A "-" bar means leads with no Lead Source. Make sure every web form, import and integration sets it, or this report will always have a blind spot.

Watch for

Recent leads lower the conversion rate because they haven't had time to convert yet. Compare older months with each other, and don't judge the current month too early.

Lead Aging by Status

Open leads grouped by status, with the average age of each group in days. It shows where leads pile up and sit.

What to do

  • The status with the highest average age is where your process stalls. Old leads in your first status mean leads aren't being worked. Old leads in a working status mean they're being touched but not moved.
  • Set a rule for how long a lead can sit in each status before it's reassigned or closed.
  • Exclude your dead or recycled statuses from the report so they don't inflate the numbers. The install guide shows how.

Rep-Level Personal reports

My Open Leads and My Untouched Leads show each rep only their own leads.

What to do

  • Have each rep subscribe to My Untouched Leads every weekday morning, set to send only when there's at least one record. The install guide shows how.
  • Use My Open Leads, oldest first, to decide each week which leads to work, recycle or close out.
